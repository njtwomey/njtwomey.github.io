A deep time series classifier computes a representation of every time point and then averages them
all together before the classifier sees anything. Whatever the model knew about where in the signal
the evidence sat is destroyed in that one step. Recovering it means a post-hoc method such as SHAP,
which needs hundreds of forward passes per explanation and, on the synthetic benchmark the
paper builds, scores worse than random.

The information does not need recovering, because it was never missing. Replacing global average
pooling with a multiple instance learning operator keeps a prediction at every time point and
aggregates those instead, so an explanation falls out of the same forward pass as the prediction
rather than being reconstructed afterwards. It is a change to one layer, which is what lets it drop
into existing backbones instead of requiring new ones.

Across 85 UCR datasets, swapping average pooling for the Conjunctive operator proposed here moved
mean accuracy from 0.841 to 0.846, so the explanations cost nothing in prediction, and they arrive
over 800 times faster than SHAP's. Which of the four pooling operators to reach for, and a synthetic
dataset built so that the discriminatory regions are known in advance, are in the paper.

This paper is explicit about where the effect stops. Interpretability falls as backbones grow more
complex, and the method improves on average pooling for FCN and InceptionTime but not for ensembles
of ResNets. Whether that traces to residual connections, to ensembling, or to the two interacting is
left open, and the answer would help decide which backbone to reach for when localisation matters as
much as accuracy.
