A smart home is a house wired with motion and door sensors, and a model over them trying to say what
the resident is doing. Time of day is a strong clue, and the usual way of supplying it is an
hour-of-day feature appended to the sensor readings. Narrowing those twenty-four bins recovers the
resolution they discard and leaves the real problem untouched, because the index runs from 0 to 23
and then stops, so a bedtime at ten to midnight and one at ten past land at opposite ends of it.

The day has no ends, so the scale should not have one either. This paper puts the time of an
activity on a circle of period twenty-four and draws it from a wrapped normal, which is an ordinary
normal wound round that circle with a location and a spread but no first hour and no last. An
activity with two habitual times needs a mixture of them, and mixtures of wrapped normals are not
identifiable. So we approximate each one by a row of ordinary normals spaced one period apart, the
central component pinned inside the period and the rest standing in for the wrapped tails. Every one
of them is an ordinary normal, so expectation propagation applies where the exact model left only
sampling.

Against Gibbs sampling on generated mixtures it converged in about a fifth of the computation time
on the small sets and half on the large. It estimated the moments more accurately on the small sets
and less accurately on the large. Expectation propagation also yields the model evidence, which
chooses the number of components, and over the sixteen models fitted for every pairing of a true and
a candidate count from one to four the highest evidence landed on the true count every time. Fitted
to three months of a single-resident apartment, Dress comes out as two modes, one near eleven in the
morning and one a few minutes past midnight. The rose diagrams for the other activities are in the
paper, with the case where two close true modes under a large variance leave the evidence preferring
a single component.

Nothing here measures the feature the model was built to replace. This paper expects an improvement
over an hour-of-day feature and runs no experiment putting the two side by side. What a better clock
is worth to an activity classifier is the open question, and the log probability of an activity
given its timestamp is the feature that would settle it. The gain should sit with the activities
that straddle midnight, which is conjecture while nothing here trains a classifier.
