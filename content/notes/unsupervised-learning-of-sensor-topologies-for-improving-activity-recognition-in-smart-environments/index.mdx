A smart home covers its walls with motion sensors and reads which ones fired to say what the
residents are doing. Motion in the kitchen fits cooking as readily as it fits walking through, so
what separates activities is which sensors fire together. Two residents break that, because a
classifier free to combine anything reads kitchen motion alongside bedroom motion as one activity
when it is two people doing two things. Ruling that pair out needs a map of the house, and drawing
one by hand means surveying every home in a deployment.

The residents survey the house themselves by walking around in it. Two sensors either side of a
doorway fire within a few seconds of each other, because the same person passed both, so a cross
correlation over every pair of streams, weighted to decay exponentially with lag, ranks pairs by
how quickly one follows the other. Mutual information over the same pairs is the second estimator,
and neither threshold uses a label. What both recover is reachability rather than proximity, which
is why the maps respect walls.

The maps never link the two residents' bedrooms, although sleeping and working in them are the most
frequent activities in the dataset, and they connect the top and bottom of the staircase across two
storeys. Cross correlation scores an area under the ROC curve of 0.879 against a hand-drawn
adjacency matrix, computed from three months of data in under two seconds. Restricting the
classifier to the sensors the map calls adjacent takes F1 from 0.632 to 0.675, while combining
everything takes it to 0.521. The CRF construction and two further testbeds are in the paper.

That settles less than it looks. Friedman and Nemenyi comparisons at a significance level of 0.01
separate the exhaustive representation from everything else and cannot separate the three adjacency
models from the single-sensor baseline, so what the topology is shown to buy is protection from the
spurious combinations rather than a gain over plain sensors. The maps also leave the door sensors
linked to nothing, presumably because a door sensor fires only when somebody opens it while a
motion sensor fires whenever anybody passes, and nothing here tests that.
