A learner model watches which questions a student answered correctly and estimates how able they
are, and the material offered next follows from that estimate. Roughly 15% of the UK population is
neurodivergent, and a dyslexic student who fails a text-heavy item has been tested on delivery as
much as on ability. Item Response Theory has one explanation for a wrong answer, so it charges that
failure to ability and recommends more of what caused it.

A wrong answer needs a second explanation. A zero-inflated model splits it into an ordinary path,
where ability loses to difficulty, and a structural path weighted by the student's conditions and
the item's delivery and response types. A low learning quality factor is a high structural weight,
and it measures the contextual inequity in a pairing of student and item, so the confounder gets
its own parameter rather than loading onto ability.

Item Response Theory, Knowledge Tracing Machines and the zero-inflated version score 0.734, 0.742
and 0.753 for accuracy, and nothing in that spread decides between them. Their Pearson correlations
with the true abilities are 0.839, 0.955 and 0.993, and the Item Response Theory error has a direction
as well as a size, because it concentrates on students with one or two neurodivergent conditions
and points downwards. Difficulty and discrimination recovery, and the effect of actively choosing a
delivery format, are in the paper.

All of it runs on simulated students, because datasets that identify neurodivergent learners and
record how each item was delivered are not available. We set the weights linking each condition to
each format by hand, and this paper calls that specification a subjective process. The recovery
gap should survive on real data, because it comes from the model having somewhere to put format
effects rather than from the simulator sharing its form, and that is conjecture until such a
dataset exists.
