Search is two stages because the function that ranks best cannot be run over a whole
corpus. <Ref id="khattab2020colbert" /> put that at 10.7 s for BERT-base over a thousand candidates against 61
ms for a cheaper scorer. So a first stage sweeps millions of documents for a few hundred candidates, and a
second reads that shortlist and orders it. I worked on ranking for recipe search a few years
ago <Ref id="takiguchi2021multifield" paren /> and stopped following it after that. A friend asked me over
dinner how re-ranking had changed and I had nothing to say. I knew the retrievers had got stronger (everybody
knew that), but not what it had done to the division between the two.

The change moved the decision upstream. <Ref id="meng2024ranked" /> held the re-ranker fixed and swapped only
the first stage, and the depth at which re-ranking stops helping moved with it: still climbing at a thousand
candidates behind `BM25`, and peaking at twenty behind a strong learned sparse retriever. Same second stage,
fifty times fewer candidates, depending only on what feeds it.

I swept depth on SciFact <Ref id="wadden2020fact" paren /> to see how far that generalises. It judges 300
queries over 5,183 abstracts, so you can sweep the whole thing end to end. `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, `BM25` and
`bge-small-en-v1.5` each supplied candidates, and one cross-encoder, `ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`, re-ranked all
three (holding the second stage fixed is what attributes a change to the retriever). Every number is nDCG@10.

<DepthPlot />

The three arms converge at the deep end, landing within 0.0007 of each other at 0.683. By then the re-ranker
has re-scored nearly everything any of them would have returned, so the ranking is entirely its own. That
figure is what it scores unconstrained, and each retriever's fate follows from which side of it that retriever
starts on. `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` at 0.645 gains from depth, `BM25` at 0.676 sits close enough to do neither, and
`bge-small-en-v1.5` at 0.713 loses 0.030 (the only movement on that arm whose interval excludes zero).

Behind the strongest of the three the solid line never reaches its own dashed baseline, so the second stage
can come out. What you are left with is a dense retriever serving its own ranking, paying once to vectorise
the corpus and then vectorising a query and taking its neighbours. The architecture barely moves, and what
differs is how the two fail: `BM25` tokenises "curry, but not too spicy", drops the stopwords, and searches
for "curry spicy". The encoder reads the string in sequence, so the qualification survives into the vector.

A hundred candidates is inherited, not measured. On this collection it was roughly right behind `BM25`, three
times deeper than `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` needed, and past the point of harm behind `bge-small-en-v1.5`. The two
stages are trained, benchmarked and swapped apart (they have been separate models built by separate teams for
as long as the architecture has existed), but the output of the system is not separable in the same way. Where
the crossing point sits once the second stage is a current re-ranker, and not a 22.7M checkpoint from 2021, is
what this does not say, and it is the number anyone holding one would want first.
