Anomaly detection picks out the members of a collection that do not look like the rest, when nobody
has said what the rest looks like. A light curve is the brightness of a star over time, and this
catalogue holds 9,236 of them across three types of periodic variable star, an unknown number filed
under the wrong type. Fitting a model of the normal ones is the obvious response, and its training
set comes from that same uncounted catalogue, so the model learns the anomalies and stops flagging
them.

Normal is a shape shared across curves rather than any one curve. A Gaussian process is a
distribution over functions rather than a single fitted curve, so it holds a whole family of shapes
at once, and stacking two of them says exactly that: one draw fixes the underlying shape a star type
follows, and each observed star is a second draw around that shape. Individual variation becomes
part of the description instead of noise to be averaged out. Scoring a new curve is then only asking
how probable it is under that description, which is its negative log marginal likelihood.

Both baselines score a curve by cross correlation against prototype centroids, which means fixing a
distance measure and a number of clusters before starting. On the star catalogue they reached 0.44
and 0.16 precision against 0.99 for the hierarchical model. Precision was still 0.98 with the model
fitted on roughly seven curves, few enough to look at individually before training, and ten times as
much data added 0.01. The final model has no periodic kernel, which we had expected to help, and the
paper says why.

Every precision figure here rests on a class held out as the outlier class, because the catalogue
this work is aimed at carries no anomaly count to divide by. For that catalogue this paper ranks the
curves and shows the strangest in each class. Scoring a detector where nobody knows the answer is
what this leaves unresolved. Refitting on the least anomalous 80% of a validation set holds
precision, and retaining more than that loses it, which the paper offers as a hint at the outlier
proportion rather than a measurement. Nobody has a catalogue with a verified count to check it
against.
