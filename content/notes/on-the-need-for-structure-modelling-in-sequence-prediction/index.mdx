Some prediction problems attach a label to every position in a sequence rather than one to the
whole, such as marking the syllable breaks in a word. A conditional random field lets neighbouring
predictions influence one another, while logistic regression predicts each position alone and reads
its neighbours as features instead. Nothing in the literature settles which one a dataset needs, so
the question gets answered by fitting both, which costs the expense the choice was meant to avoid.

The auto-correlations answer it before either model is fitted. The excess risk incurred by ignoring
the structure is tied to how fast a sequence's auto-correlations decay, and where that decay is
geometric a classifier can be learned which behaves in the limit as though the data were
independent. The two also differ in cost whatever a dataset turns out to need. A linear-chain conditional random field carries more parameters by the square of the label count, and its exact marginals arrive only once a sequence has ended, where logistic regression gives them at every position as it goes.

Word hyphenation, smart home activity recognition and a synthetic sequence of loaded die rolls all
came out indistinguishable under baseline features. Hyphenation scored 0.963 against 0.966 in favour
of logistic regression, and a two-way analysis of variance found no main effect significant at one percent, which leaves the cheaper model ahead on Occam's razor. Its auto-correlations run larger at positive lags than negative ones, so we weighted the
templates towards the following substrings and improved both models on all ten folds. The mirror
image emphasising the past degraded every metric instead. The activity recognition gains and the
bounds are in the paper.

The die rolls cut the other way. There the conditional random field peaks on three feature templates
and overfits as more are added, because the structure encodes a simple generative process in fewer
parameters than the features needed to match it. We picked the templates from those profiles by
hand, and automating that is the open problem. Every correlation in those profiles is a linear one.
Sequential data plausibly has a fundamental bandwidth, a span over which one position has marked
influence on the rest, which this paper offers as speculation.
